# Arquivo: B.yml
title: Kerberoasting via RC4 TGS Request Detected in Zeek Kerberos Logs
id: a7b3e1f4-2c8d-4a9e-b5f1-3d7c2e4b8a96
status: experimental
description: >
  Detects a Kerberos Ticket-Granting Service (TGS) request that negotiates the
  weak RC4-HMAC cipher, as observed in Zeek kerberos.log. Modern Active Directory
  environments default to AES-256 or AES-128 for Kerberos service tickets; an
  explicit RC4 request by a user principal is a strong behavioral indicator of a
  Kerberoasting attack, where the adversary deliberately downgrades encryption to
  obtain an offline-crackable service ticket. The detection excludes requests
  originating from machine (computer) accounts — identified by a trailing dollar
  sign in the client principal — which routinely generate RC4 TGS traffic and
  represent benign baseline noise. Successful ticket issuance is required to
  confirm that the downgrade was accepted by the KDC.
references:
  - https://adsecurity.org/?p=3458
author: Senior Threat Detection Engineer
date: 2026-05-25
tags:
  - attack.credential_access
  - attack.t1558.003
logsource:
  product: zeek
  service: kerberos
detection:
  selection:
    request_type: 'TGS'
    cipher: 'rc4-hmac'
    success: 'true'
  filter_machine_accounts:
    client|endswith: '$'
  condition: selection and not filter_machine_accounts
falsepositives:
  - Legacy applications and services that are explicitly configured to require
    RC4 Kerberos encryption and have not been migrated to AES, resulting in
    normal enterprise SPN activity appearing as suspicious.
  - Network appliances (e.g., NetApp, older NAS devices) or third-party services
    that do not support AES Kerberos and therefore always negotiate RC4.
  - Cross-forest Kerberos authentication where inter-forest trusts have not been
    configured for AES, causing all cross-realm TGS requests to use RC4.
  - Automated testing or vulnerability scanning tools that probe Kerberos
    encryption support as part of an authorized assessment.
level: medium
